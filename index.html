Hi. I'm Christopher Miotto.

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Michigan, where I work as a Graduate Research Assistant. Prior to joining U-M, I earned my Bachelor's degree with Highest Distinction and my Master's degree in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Purdue University. During my time at Purdue, I developed a strong foundation in hardware and systems through my work as a researcher in the SoCET lab and as a Graduate Teaching Assistant for undergraduate engineering courses.

My research is centered on computer architecture, with a specific focus on dataflow spatial architectures and hardware/software co-design. I am passionate about exploring how complex systems can be optimized from the compiler level down to the microarchitecture. Ultimately, my goal is to drive meaningful innovations in processing efficiency and architectural design for next-generation computing systems.

Current Research

Advancing computer architecture through hardware/software co-design.

My current research focuses on exploring dataflow spatial architectures and hardware/software co-design. I am particularly interested in how complex systems can be optimized across the computing stack, from compiler transformations down to GPU microarchitecture and coarse-grained reconfigurable architectures (CGRAs). By analyzing these interactions, my work aims to drive meaningful innovations in processing efficiency and architectural design for next-generation computing systems.

Academic Projects

A selection of my microarchitecture and hardware design implementations.

GPGPU-Sim Microarchitecture Enhancements

Engineered a Register File Cache and modified warp scheduling to bypass arbiter MRF reads and reduce warp demotions.

gem5 Multiprocessor Ruby Coherence

Implemented RegionScout by engineering C++ NSRT and CRH structures to extend the MOESI_hammer coherence protocol and suppress control broadcasts.

gem5 Microarchitecture O3 Enhancements

Engineered an O3 Waiting Instruction Buffer and a Strict Exclusive victim cache to divert L2 miss dependents and resolve pipeline stalls.

Dual-Core 5-Stage-Pipelined RISC-V Processor

Designed a multicore processor in SystemVerilog featuring split caches and MSI cache coherence.
